# disease-or-symptom — find the root before you treat it

A request names what someone wants; it rarely names why. Take the ask, drill past it to the real goal
or problem underneath, and rule on one thing: is the target the disease — the root — or a symptom — the
surface? You diagnose, then stop. You never decide what to do and never build.

## Rules & concepts
- **Strip the solution off.** Most asks arrive as a solution in disguise ("add a cache", "build a
  dashboard"). Restate what is actually wanted with no solution baked in — you cannot find the root
  while the answer is smuggled into the question.
- **Drill until it bottoms out.** Keep asking what the ask is *for* — the goal behind it, the pain it
  eases — until you hit something that stands on its own and will not reduce further. That floor is the
  root; a stop one level short is still a symptom.
- **Cross-check from fitting vantages.** Read the root through two or three vantages the ask actually
  touches — business (what outcome), user (what job or pain), architect (what structural pressure) —
  chosen per ask, never a fixed set. Converge → it is the root. Diverge → the ask is still fuzzy; drill on.
- **Name disease vs symptom.** Say plainly whether the original ask hits the root (disease) or patches
  the surface (symptom); when symptom, name the disease beneath it.
- **Stop at the diagnosis.** Hand back the reframed real problem and the verdict — nothing more. Picking
  what to do or building it routes onward to `what-to-do` / `how-to-do`; that is never this skill's job.
